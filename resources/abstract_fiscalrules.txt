This paper studies the optimal design of fiscal rules for an economy subject to sovereign default risk with a pegged exchange rate. When the peg is modeled as a simple increase in income volatility (e.g., because the exchange rate cannot act as a shock-absorber), the economy prefers a tighter fiscal rule. This simple result is misleading: when the increase in income volatility is created by amplification through nominal rigidities, the optimal rule remains much closer to its floating exchange-rate benchmark. In a calibration to Ecuador (a dollarized economy), we find that the changes in the calibration (particularly, in patience and costs of default) compensate for the effect of different intensity of nominal rigidities. An economy with a pegged exchange rate and one which floats, when calibrated to match the same levels of debt and spreads, end up with similar optimal fiscal rules. However, the causal effect on optimal fiscal rules of increasing the volatility of shocks remains orders of magnitude larger than the effect of increasing the strength of nominal rigidities. These results then recommend caution in applying reduced-form estimates to calibrate optimal fiscal rules.
